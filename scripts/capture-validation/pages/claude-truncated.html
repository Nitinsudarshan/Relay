I want to design a capture system that acquires the underlying source of a page rather than a picture of it. The hard part is not reading the DOM, it is knowing how much of the document the DOM actually represents at the moment you read it. Virtualized lists unmount what scrolls past. Lazily-loaded media has no source until it approaches the viewport. Network-paged history arrives only when you reach a boundary, and reaching the boundary moves it. Collapsed sections may or may not hold their content. Each of those needs a different answer, and treating them as one problem produces either a thin capture or a browser agent that clicks things nobody asked it to click. What I want written down is which mechanism applies where, what the least invasive way to obtain each one is, and — most importantly — what the capture is then allowed to claim about its own completeness.
